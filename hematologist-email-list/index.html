Buying a small-specialty list

What a Small Specialty Needs from a List

Hematology is a compact field, so a careless list wastes a large share of every send. Competing United States pages usually give no record count, collapse every blood specialist into a single label and describe their checks in one line. For a specialty this narrow, those gaps hurt more than they would in a broad one.

Start with a visible count. A buyer should know whether a file holds hundreds of doctors or thousands, and Infinyx Global's file holds 11,000+. Then check subspecialty labels. A sickle cell program and a lymphoma service buy different things, so each record carries its own tag, and setting labels separate academic centers, community practices and infusion sites that follow different buying paths.

Precision matters more in rare disease. A hemophilia gene therapy reaches only a handful of treatment centers in any region, and aiming at the wrong subspecialty means a message that no recipient can act on. Tags for subspecialty and setting let a rare disease team build a short, accurate target list and spend its effort on conversations.

Geography works differently too. Hematologists cluster around academic medical centers and large cancer programs, with community practices serving surrounding regions. A territory manager can use state and metro filters to map those clusters, then plan travel, events and mailed materials around them. One accuracy figure applies, 97% deliverability, and the build method is explained in the next section.

Timing is the last difference worth checking. Launch messages work four to six weeks before a therapy reaches the market, trial recruitment belongs at the opening of an enrollment window, and laboratory offers do best ahead of contract renewals. Education events need about two months of notice.

Mistakes are easy to make in a small field. Treating the specialty as one audience mixes benign and malignant hematology, ignoring setting sends the same call to action to an owner and an investigator, and mailing during society meetings buries the message.
